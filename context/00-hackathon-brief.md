## The single most important fact

> "Colosseum hackathons are **startup competitions**."

There are **two published rubrics and they disagree**. Optimise for the second one.

**Rubric A — the legal PDF, §8.** Functionality & code quality · Potential impact/TAM ·
Novelty · UX · Open-source & composability · Business plan.

**Rubric B — the FAQ, "How will submissions be judged?"** This is the one the team
actually runs:

- **Founder + Market Fit** — why *this* team for *this* market
- **Insight** — a non-obvious understanding of the problem, or a new tech/trend creating an opening
- **Product + Execution** — does it work, vs. competition, shipping velocity
- **Potential Market Size** — large, or small and growing fast
- **Founder Communication** — can you explain the vision and grow a userbase
- **Viability** — scalable, sustainable business
- **Traction** — real demand or revenue already

On the GitHub repo they say they want to see: significant work done *during* the
hackathon, done *by you*, with strategic prioritisation. And explicitly **not**:
a particular language or framework, specific design patterns, best practices, or
code-quality checks.

**Implication:** a beautiful codebase with no story loses to a rough product with a
sharp insight, a named customer, and a clear market. Budget time accordingly.

## Process after the deadline

Colosseum team runs multiple evaluation rounds → shortlist goes to the judging panel →
a smaller group gets a **15-minute Zoom interview** → winners. Roughly one month.

You are optimising to survive a *first-pass skim*, then to be compelling in a
15-minute live conversation.

## Hard rules

- **One team per person. One submission per team.** No hedging across projects.
- Judged **only on work completed between Sep 14 and Oct 12**. Pre-existing code is
  allowed but **must be disclosed** — misrepresentation means disqualification, a ban
  from future Colosseum hackathons, and revoked prizes.
- "Pre-existing code" does *not* mean other people's open source. Composing with
  existing protocols is explicitly encouraged.
- For new startups that haven't raised significant capital. Not for established
  companies.
- All content in English. Solo entries allowed; teams recommended.
- Register at colosseum.com before the deadline; every team member needs an account
  and the team leader adds them at submission.
- Ineligible: sanctioned jurisdictions (see rules §3(b)), under-18s without a
  case-by-case exception, employees of Colosseum/sponsors.

## Submission checklist

- [ ] Product name + brief description
- [ ] Which blockchains and tools are integrated ← **this is how you enter a track**
- [ ] All teammates with backgrounds; team location
- [ ] Product logo or graphic
- [ ] GitHub repo — open-source encouraged; private allowed if `hackathon@colosseum.com` has access
- [ ] **2–3 min presentation video** — "one of the first resources judges review"
- [ ] **≤3 min product demo video** — how the product actually works
- [ ] Go-to-market strategy, demand validation, distribution plans

Two separate videos. The presentation video is the pitch; the demo video is the proof.
They are reviewed *before* the code.

**Weekly updates:** a concise 1-minute video each week. Not strictly required —
"strongly recommended for anyone serious about competing." Cheap signal of momentum
to the people who build the shortlist. Do them.

## Multi-track eligibility

Track prizes are "awarded in addition to" the overall awards, and the FAQ says every
submission is eligible for prizes "regardless of the crypto infrastructure used."
The submission form asks which chains you integrate — plural.

Nothing published says a single submission can be considered for two *track* pools.
**Treat that as unverified.** Design for one primary track you can win outright; if a
second integration is genuinely load-bearing for the product, it is upside, not a
plan. Ask in the Colosseum Discord before betting on it.

## Cross-cutting facts worth remembering

- **Phantom CASH** is both the prize currency and a recommended integration. Phantom's
  pitch: CASH is "accepted across Stripe's global merchant network" and "lets you keep
  100% of the net revenue generated from the supply you originate." That last clause is
  a ready-made, defensible answer to *Viability* — a real revenue line, not a hand-wave.
- **Tempo is incubated by Stripe and Paradigm**, and Phantom CASH settles across
  Stripe's merchant network. The Stripe thread runs through this hackathon.
- Judges include people from Phantom, Base, Arcium, Espresso, Anza, Ethlabs, MetaDAO,
  Realms, Drift, Ellipsis Labs. Privacy and DeFi infrastructure are well represented.
- Track record: Frontier (Spring 2026) drew 2,858 projects; Cypherpunk 1,576;
  Breakout 1,416. Expect ~2,500–3,000 submissions. Ten Solana winners out of that.
