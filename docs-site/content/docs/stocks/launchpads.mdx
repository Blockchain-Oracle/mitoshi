Tempo makes tokens a native primitive: a predeployed **TIP-20 factory** issues them, and fees can be paid in any USD stablecoin. Mitoshi's two launchpads build on that.

## Stablecoin

One batch from your account creates a USD TIP-20 quoted in pathUSD, mints its supply, places a pair of flip orders on Tempo's enshrined stablecoin DEX around par, seeds its fee-AMM pool so the new coin can pay network fees, and records it in **LaunchRegistry**. The batch can be signed by a one-tap access key: the factory's `createToken` is a call, not a contract creation.

On 29 September a root key created "Mitoshi Root Test Dollar" in one transaction, with its pair orders resting and its fee pool funded, and then paid a transaction's fee in the new coin; an access key did the same for a second coin ([evidence](/help/evidence)).

## Memecoin

**CurveLaunchpad** launches a TIP-20 whose currency is its symbol (fiat codes and stock tickers are reserved). Traders buy and sell against a bonding curve that mints on buy and burns on sell. When the curve's reserve reaches its threshold the launch is complete, and anyone may **graduate** it into a hookless Uniswap v4 pool whose full-range position the **LiquidityLocker** holds.

Until graduation a compound transfer policy keeps the coin out of the PoolManager, so nobody can seed the migration pool early. A pool squatted at the wrong price is corrected at graduation, or graduation moves to a backup fee tier with the reason. Payouts go to the caller only, each after a receive-policy check.

## On testnet today

Both are deployed on Moderato and their live drives passed: a stablecoin with its pair and fee pool in one transaction, and a memecoin template, launch, creator buy, squatted tier and graduation. The ops **launch-keeper** that would graduate complete launches plans and simulates only — it has no sender wired yet. <AppLink href="/launch">Launch</AppLink> and its pages are registered and not built.
